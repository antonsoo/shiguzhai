## Design Context

### Audience and use

The creator asks for English-speaking learners, ancient musical traditions and
complex inventions that teach something surprising. They explicitly reject
generic catalogues and superficial showcase ideas. This project uses the
published measurements of a particular ancient Chinese bell set to hear two
tones from one object, inspect ambiguous intervals and compare a transmitted
Chinese proportion rule with measured dimensions.

### Chosen identity

A listening bench beside an archaeological measurement sheet. Bone-white paper,
charcoal ink, cinnabar for the lower resonance and muted blue-green for the upper.
Large direct sans typography, traditional Chinese characters as precise labels,
thin ruled tables and one generous modal drawing. A real museum photograph is
clearly a separate object from the measured bells. No simulated museum recordings,
decorative calligraphy, dashboard cards or claims of predictive finite elements.

### Principles

1. One object can support two independent bending patterns; location changes coupling.
2. Play and preserve the published frequencies before trying a modern semitone fit.
3. Missing museum measurements remain missing; no inferred pitches or filled slots.
4. Read Chinese vocabulary through the dimension or operation it names.
5. Keep measured data, modern sound synthesis and schematic mode shapes distinct.
6. Every operation works by keyboard, at phone width, and in a retained offline study.

### Existing exhibit integration

Shuang is a second room within Shiguzhai, using a different measured set and
a craft text. Retain the original exhibit and label every source set separately.
Use direct links between rooms and one complete offline edition.
